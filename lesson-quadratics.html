Quadratic Functions

Understand the idea, see why it works, follow a complete example, then test your understanding.

1. The core idea

Mathematics becomes reliable when each step follows from a property that remains true. In this lesson, the central relationship is:

f(x) = x² - 4x + 3
Why this works

A quadratic has constant second differences because the change in its slope is constant. Completing the square reveals the vertex by rewriting the same expression in a translated square form.

2. Worked example

Follow the reasoning one step at a time. Each transformation keeps the mathematical meaning intact.

Factor the quadratic
x² - 4x + 3 = (x-1)(x-3)
Find zeros
x = 1 and x = 3
Find axis of symmetry
x = (1+3)/2 = 2
Evaluate at x=2
f(2) = -1, so vertex is (2,-1)
